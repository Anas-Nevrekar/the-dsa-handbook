152. Maximum_Product_Subarray
Difficulty: Medium
Problem Understanding
a. The goal is to find a contiguous subarray within a given array of numbers.
b. The product of all numbers within this specific subarray must be the largest possible.
c. The subarray must contain at least one number.

Approach
a. The solution iterates through the array, keeping track of the maximum and minimum product of any subarray ending at the current position.
b. It is crucial to track both maximum and minimum products because a negative number multiplied by another negative number can turn a small minimum into a large maximum.
c. When a positive number is encountered, it extends the current maximum and minimum products naturally.
d. When a negative number is encountered, it swaps the roles of the maximum and minimum products: multiplying a negative number by the current maximum might yield a new minimum, while multiplying it by the current minimum might yield a new maximum.
e. If a zero is encountered, it resets the current maximum and minimum products to 1, effectively starting a new subarray product calculation, as zero makes any product zero.
f. A global variable maintains the overall maximum product found across all subarrays processed so far.

Algorithm Steps
a. Initialize `cur_max` and `cur_min` to 1, representing the product of an empty subarray.
b. Initialize a global maximum product variable `ans` to the smallest possible integer value (`INT_MIN`).
c. Iterate through the input array once to initially update `ans` with the maximum of `ans` and each individual number. This handles cases where the maximum product is a single element, including zero.
d. Begin a second iteration through each number `n` in the input array.
e. If `n` is 0, reset both `cur_max` and `cur_min` to 1. Then skip to the next number.
f. Store the values of `n * cur_max` and `n * cur_min` in temporary variables before updating `cur_max` and `cur_min`. This is because `cur_max` and `cur_min` will be updated based on their previous values.
g. Update `cur_max` to be the maximum among three values: `n` itself, the product of `n` and the previous `cur_max`, and the product of `n` and the previous `cur_min`.
h. Update `cur_min` to be the minimum among three values: `n` itself, the product of `n` and the previous `cur_max`, and the product of `n` and the previous `cur_min`.
i. Update the global `ans` by taking the maximum between the current `ans` and the newly calculated `cur_max`.
j. After the loop finishes, `ans` will hold the maximum product subarray.

Time Complexity
O(N)

Space Complexity
O(1)
